Tim (seahag prompt log #184): a year slider for the map's measures with years, so readers can see
which years a change covers and measure enrollment change over other spans; a Play button that runs a
one-year measure from its first year to its latest; figures that round to zero shown without a sign;
Education's share of spending on the map.

Changes:

1. export_explorer_data.py, map_series() (new), called from map_data(): the map's measures year by year,
   along FY2005 to the cap tab's latest year (FY2027), trailing years without a value left off.
   Towns: spending per resident (spend_total / pop), population, equalized value per resident, room
   under the levy limit, the single-family bill's share of income, education's share of spending,
   residents per square mile (population over land area), and the cap's change per household with no
   cap. That last: each history year's no-cap target is cey times the year's total target over its
   total cey (as cap_data's factor and the cap tab's year-by-year chart), over that year's ACS
   households, FY2009 on; FY2026 and FY2027 are the cap tab's own figures, so the latest year matches
   map.json. Districts: in-district spending per pupil, in-district enrollment, actual spending above
   required, Chapter 70 aid, and required spending (aid plus required local), each per foundation
   pupil, and students per square mile (foundation enrollment over the member towns' land area, as
   district_types counts members; the last year is the community type's own FY2026 figure). cpi along
   the same years; "w" on each split town: its pupils in its elementary and its secondary district,
   the weights map_data already used for "all".

2. Written to its own file, src/data/massppe/map-years.json (620 KB, 248 KB gzipped): the site's map
   draws from map.json and loads the years after. map.json is unchanged but for one new figure,
   "edu" (education's share, each town's latest year), from the new edu_share(t), which map_series
   also uses; it's the explorer tile's rule (education over all functions, per resident).

3. The site computes growth and change in the browser on the years chosen, by map_data's rules
   (trend growth before and after inflation, each fitted on its own; change between the first and
   last positive values; a split town's two districts combined by "w").
