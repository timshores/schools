2026-10-22 (Session 14) - Explorer map: towns and districts colored by how they've changed

Tim: add a Massachusetts choropleth to the Commonwealth tab, showing towns and districts
colored by interesting trends (after the scatterplot tiles were cut to the pairs with the
fewest cautions, and he wondered how valuable the scatterplots are).

Changes:

1. update_town_boundaries.py (new): the Census Bureau's 2023 cartographic boundary file of
   county subdivisions (1:500,000, clipped to the shoreline), read with the standard library
   (a small .shp/.dbf reader; no geographic packages in the venv). Projected equirectangularly at
   the state's middle latitude into a 1600 x 993 box and rounded to whole units (~190 m), which
   drops close points and keeps shared borders identical, so neighbours still meet. Writes
   data/town_boundaries.json: 351 towns, 17,328 points, 103 KB. Ten towns that became cities
   have two codes in census_town_population.csv; each takes whichever the outlines use, and the
   file records the population file's town name for the join.

2. export_explorer_data.py map_data() writes map.json (205 KB): each town's outline; the
   district serving its elementary grades (its own district, else its regional one; of two
   regionals, the one with fewer towns) and its high school grades (its regional district, of two
   the larger, else its own); and measures, each with its years:
   towns - spending per resident and EQV per resident, yearly growth in FY2025 dollars;
   population change; room under the levy limit (unused capacity over the limit); single-family
   bill over income per capita; FY2027 change per household with no cap (cap.json).
   districts - in-district spending per pupil, yearly growth in FY2025 dollars; in-district FTE
   change; spending above required over required; Chapter 70 aid over required spending.
   Town patterns: 185 own district only, 108 one regional, 44 own + regional, 9 two regionals,
   5 none (Gosnold, Monroe, Mount Washington, New Ashford, Tyringham).

3. Explorer (mywebsite map.ts): the map leads the Commonwealth tab. Towns / School districts,
   and for districts Elementary / High school; a measure menu; a paragraph on each measure with
   how the towns or districts split; five classes (a change diverges around zero, orange down,
   gray near none, blue up; a level runs light to dark blue); hatched for no value; hover
   outlines the town, or every town of its district; a click opens it in its tab; a table of
   every value; enlargeable.

   First readings: 292 of 351 towns' spending per resident grew faster than prices, FY2009-
   FY2025; 239 of 241 high school districts' spending per pupil did; the no-cap map is blue on the
   Cape and Islands and around Boston, orange across the interior.

4. Grades: Elementary / Secondary / All (Tim: "High school" didn't make sense). map_data() now
   gives the 48 towns split between two districts an "all" set: the two combined, each weighted
   by the town's own pupils in it (a district of its own: all its foundation pupils; a regional
   one: the town's foundation enrollment there, from dese_c70_regional_members.csv, FY2025 only).
   Spending against the requirement combines at the latest year both districts reported (five
   towns' own districts last reported FY2024: Brewster, Dover, Eastham, Orleans, Wellfleet).
   Leverett, spending above required: its own district 76%, Amherst-Pelham 34%, combined 52%.
   The map opens on school districts, all grades, spending above the requirement.

5. Amherst check (Tim: the town page said +0.5% a year after inflation, the map -1.5%). The map
   is right: $1,863 per resident in FY2009 to $2,108 in FY2025 while prices rose 44%, -1.5% a
   year in FY2025 dollars. The town page's +0.5% was the nominal trend (and +0.8% the nominal
   endpoint growth); neither said so. The tile and the trend now say "before inflation" in
   nominal dollars, as they said "after inflation" in FY2025 dollars.
