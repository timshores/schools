2026-11-05 (Session 21) - Explorer Districts: Budgeted and Actual Net School Spending, Students

Tim (seahag prompt log #205, #206): level-services budgets for each district, to see districts approving
budgets that fall short of or exceed them, and students by demographics, as DESE's school profiles show.
Level services isn't collected by the state (each district works it out, and defines it, for itself), so the
budget panel uses what DESE does collect: each district's approved budget against what it spent.

Changes:

1. update_dese_nss_budgets.py (new): DESE's net school spending compliance summaries. Each year's workbook gives
   every district's required and actual NSS for the year and its required and budgeted NSS for the next, from
   its End-of-Year Report. DESE links FY2021 on (comply-fy20XX.xlsx); FY2010 to FY2020, under older names
   (comply10.xls ... comply19.xlsx, comply-fy2020.xlsx), survive only on the Internet Archive, some only in later
   captures (an earlier capture can be DESE's HTML page; is_workbook() checks the file's first bytes). Archived
   copies are kept in data/dese_nss_compliance/ and reused. Columns found by their header words, which moved over
   the years. Writes data/dese_nss_budgets.csv (one row per district and year: the budget from the year before's
   file, the actual from the year's own) and dese_nss_budget_sources.json (each file's address and capture).
   Budgets FY2011-FY2026, 333 districts.

   Checks: Leverett FY2026 budget $2,924,227 (FY2025 file); statewide, actual NSS runs 0.2-0.9% under budget
   every year FY2011-FY2025; Amherst and Amherst-Pelham within 2.5% either way. Leverett spent within 0.3% of
   its budget FY2011-FY2013 and 15-17% under FY2023-FY2025: flagged to Tim, not explained.

2. update_dese_enrollment.py (new): E2C Hub dataset t8td-gens (Enrollment: Grade, Race/Ethnicity, Gender, and
   Selected Populations), district rows, SY1992-SY2026, to data/dese_enrollment.csv. A year with no students
   has its zero percents blanked; race percents add to 100% in every row but one (SY2008 Southeastern Regional
   Vocational, 103%), reported as a warning.

3. export_explorer_data.py: budget_fields() (budgeted NSS per foundation pupil, pooled over the districts with a
   budget and pupils; actual against budget in percent, pooled over those with both; FY2026's budget against
   FY2025's actual and budget and FY2026's requirement), student_fields() (race and ethnicity in five groups, as
   the towns' residents are grouped; students with disabilities, English learners, low income, economically
   disadvantaged, high needs; each in percent, pooled by counts; FY2026 past the axis). meta.budgets,
   meta.students. CSVs for the Data tab: districts-nss-budgets.csv, districts-students.csv.

4. mywebsite: three district panels, "Budgeted and actual net school spending" (budget, actual, required lines;
   under them, each year's spending against its budget, the comparison's as dots), "Students by race and
   ethnicity" (shares, a group's trend and comparison once chosen), "Students in selected groups" (the district's
   and the comparison's lines; low income's three measures drawn apart, with no trend). Data tab sources and
   methods.

5. Tim (#207): more map measures and correlations from the new data. export_explorer_data.py district_shares()
   (students with disabilities, English learners, low income from FY2022, high needs, students of color, special
   education's share of spending, spending against budget, as fractions) and town_shares() (residents 65 and older,
   of color, with a disability, long-term debt per resident), into map.json (latest, and for a split town the two
   districts weighted by its pupils), map-years.json (year by year), and commonwealth.json (the district shares
   and payments per out-of-district pupil). Town overrides passed since FY2005, counted from the passed votes.

   Correlations, Spearman's rho over the 288 traditional districts with 20 or more pupils, Boston and
   Provincetown left out as the Commonwealth tab does, FY2025: low income against income per capita -0.83 and the
   average tax bill -0.79 (expected); payments per out-of-district pupil against income +0.66 and low income -0.64;
   English learners and students of color against foundation enrollment +0.58 and +0.59; spending as a share of
   the requirement against low income -0.52; Chapter 70 aid per pupil against low income +0.74 (the formula's own:
   its foundation budget adds for low-income students). Spending against budget is weak: FY2025's gap against
   FY2026's budget growth -0.39, which is mechanical (growth is measured from FY2025's actual, which underspending
   lowers), and the five-year average gap against foundation enrollment +0.32 (smaller districts spend further
   under budget).
   Tested in the tab itself, every pair of 0.4 or more with a new measure: two pass its gallery test (no caution
   but shared towns) and join the gallery, spending against the requirement and the low-income share (rho -0.53,
   all district types) and students of color and enrollment (+0.56, no caution). Out-of-district payments against
   income or low income, and English learners against enrollment, show uneven spread; they stay in the picker.

9. Tim (#217): "Why would Springfield not be in a size peer group?" The ten cities whose districts are very large
   stood apart from every town average, peer groups included, so the town map left them in no group. Now every city
   and town is in a population group (the site's name for the code's peer groups): TOWN_PEERS splits the top band
   at 100,000, 50,000-99,999 (18 towns) and 100,000 or more (9: Boston, Worcester, Springfield, Cambridge, Lowell,
   Brockton, Lynn, Quincy, New Bedford), so the largest have a group of their own as the very large districts do;
   peer_of() no longer leaves them out, and the peer and region-peer averages draw on every town. They still stand
   apart from the statewide (default) and regional averages. Ids towns-50000-plus gives way to towns-50000-99999
   and towns-100000-plus.
